# Lemo-Opuscar Reel: treatment

Style: **Whiteboard Explainer** (`styles/whiteboard/STYLE.md`). 40 s, 1080×1920 vertical (9:16), 24 fps.
Voice: the user's own recording (English), cleaned and lightly edited. No TTS.
Picture locked to the user's four-part structure: 0–6 hook · 6–17 intro · 17–32 how it works · 32–40 end, with a 0.5 s breath at each boundary.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **The subtraction (chosen).** A film needs a camera, an editor and footage. The pen writes each one, then strikes it out, until only a big **0** is left. Then the board answers: one repo, 39 styles, three steps, and this film is the proof. It's a list that turns: the first beat removes everything, and every beat after it rebuilds from zero.
2. **The countdown.** A big 39 counts down while each style flashes past, landing on "1 link". Rejected: 39 counts take too long for 11 s, and it hides the "how".
3. **The loop.** Open on the end card already drawn, rewind with the eraser to a blank board, then play forward. Rejected: the rewind costs the first three seconds, and the hook has to be instant.

Why 1: it's the user's brief, and it's the strongest hook. Striking things out is something only a board can do with its own grammar: the strike is a marker stroke and it has its own sound.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A whiteboard crosses out everything a film supposedly needs, then shows that one repo and one AI agent build it from zero, and the board you just watched turns out to be that film.*

- **Setup:** Camera, Editor, Footage. Each one is written and crossed out. **0**.
- **Turn:** a blue line runs out of the 0 and the camera rides it down to "Lemo-Opuscar", whose **O** is written in the same orange as the 0 (an echo). 39 styles drop in as magnets.
- **Ending:** the camera pulls back past the board edge. The whole board is this film, and then it **rewinds and redraws itself with no pen at all** (the board that draws itself). The camera pushes down into the end card.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Dropbox explainer (2008) / Common Craft "in Plain English":** problem → product → three steps → call to action, one idea per frame, the simplest drawing that carries it.
  *Learn:* the order of information, three numbered steps with enough air, and a CTA that is a single action. *Don't take:* their characters, cut-paper look, music or wording.
- **RSA Animate (style reference):** one continuous board, the camera travels instead of cutting. *Learn:* the camera route as a map of the argument. *Don't take:* hands, drawings or the dense, busy board.

## 6. Beat sheet (to be locked to word timings once the narration text is confirmed)

The voice's own pauses already fit the structure: hook speech 1.26–5.78 s, intro 6.03–17.67 s, how-it-works 18.48–31.23 s, end 32.19–36.29 s (measured on the raw recording). Edit: the intro is 0.6 s too long for its 11 s window, so its three internal pauses (10.8, 12.0, 13.1 s) are tightened by ~0.2 s each. No time-stretching. The how-it-works and end segments start 0.5 s after their boundary.

## 7. Cue map

- 120 BPM, 4/4, one beat = 0.5 s. Boundaries land on beats: 6.0 (beat 12), 17.0 (beat 34), 32.0 (beat 64). Each 0.5 s breath is a one-beat rest.
- **Kalimba and plucked strings with a soft synth pulse** (a tech topic, kept warm), all synthesized: kalimba (modal synthesis), plucked nylon (Karplus-Strong), a soft sine-saw synth pulse, finger snaps, a felt sub.
  - R1: a single muted pluck on each struck word; a full stop into silence before the 0; the 0 lands on a low felt chord.
  - R2: the kalimba ostinato enters with the title; a bright kalimba ping on each style magnet; the "many more" burst = a fast upward kalimba run.
  - R3: **one instrument added per step**: ① + synth pulse, ② + plucked bass, ③ + finger snaps (the full machine).
  - R4: the rewind is the last two bars reversed and squeezed; the replay is a fast arpeggio; it resolves on the end card.
- Music sits about 20 dB under the voice by RMS and ducks a further 4 dB under speech. Foley sits about 12 dB under the voice.

## 9. Type and subtitles

- Board text: single-line handwriting, written in stroke order (the engine's single-line font). English only.
- The one Persian word, «ریپو», uses **Vazirmatn** (OFL), right to left and joined, revealed right-to-left by a marker wipe because there is no single-line Persian font. It is checked with screenshots.
- **No burned subtitles**: the brief asks for minimal on-screen text. A `.srt` is delivered for Instagram's caption track.
- The bottom ~14% and top ~10% of the frame are kept clear of key action (Instagram UI overlays on reels).

## 10. Rights

- "Claude Code" mark: unless the user confirms they may use the official logo, the end card uses a **hand-written "Claude Code" wordmark with a terminal `>_` glyph**, not the official artwork.
- Open-source mark: a generic `</>` + git-branch graph, not the GitHub Octocat.
- Lemo-Opuscar is a public MIT project; its name is written by hand.

## 11. Not the demo again (DIRECTOR §11)

Compared with the style's demo after the treatment: structure (a subtraction list into product → 3 steps → CTA, vs a science mystery with a twist) **differs**; opening (struck words → 0, vs a tiny pin) **differs**; signature shot (the board rewinds and redraws itself with no pen, vs a full-board pull-back) **differs**; camera path (vertical, top to bottom, vs horizontal regions) **differs**; score (kalimba, plucked strings and synth pulse, vs marimba/pizzicato clockwork) **differs**; ending (a comment-box CTA vs an end card under the title) **differs**. Shared: three-step mechanism and a pull-back, both native to the style.
